# Area 4 — Creativity of the idea and its execution

Assessed commit: `9dc326f` in `/Users/tcs16/AgenticAI/ProCyclingResults`, on 2026-09-26 (the evening of the Worlds women's road race). Read-only. Scratch files under `assessments/tools/area4/` (referred to below as `area4-scratch/`).

## 1. Summary

**Creativity of the idea: B+. Creativity of execution: B+.** The idea is a season-shaped, one-page race desk for fans rather than a rider database (ProCyclingStats) or a news feed (Cyclingnews), and its genuinely original parts are around the edges of the results: a to-scale season timeline that says "58 of 61 WorldTour races run · next: Il Lombardia, 10 Oct", a closing letter that replaces the header when the season ends, a national-championships almanac that turned 293 near-empty cards into a map and six collapsed lines, and a plain-language release-notes page that reads like a letter to the reader. The one thing that most defines the site is candour: it says only what it can show and says where it came from — hatched "typical, not confirmed" windows, a dashed pictogram labelled "no elevation profile is available" instead of a plausible silhouette, "This season on this site" on every rider card, "Not a photograph — nobody in it exists" under the committee portrait. Execution is strong wherever a decision was made from a comp with real data (calendar, almanac, hover cards, profile palette) and weakest in the parts nobody comped: the hero, whose eyebrow ("UCI-Inspired Race Desk") and feature-list tagline could top any results site, and the section copy, which describes the layout to the reader ("arranged in a three-column grid on larger screens"). The site currently speaks in three registers — marketing in the hero, engineer in the section descriptions and footer, committee on About — and only the third is its own. The engineering choice (one file, no packages, no database, documentation as the memory between agent sessions) has demonstrably bought speed and transparency, and its ceiling is now visible in a 17,112-line `server.js` with a 3,100-line stylesheet and 1,075-line client script living inside template literals.

## 3. The premise, and whether the product delivers it

**Premise:** one free page that tells a cycling fan where the season stands — what just finished, what is live, what is next — for the top tier only, drawn from public sources the site names, and never drawn prettier than the data allows.

**Delivered: yes, consistently, with one gap.** The page order on 26 September is the Worlds (because it is race week), then men's and women's WorldTour each as Recent Results → Upcoming, then the championships almanac; the calendar sits one click away and the close-out is ready for 19 October. Every card links its full placings out to PCS rather than pretending to be a database (`getRaceResultsUrl`; 108 "Full stage results", 41 "Full classification", 15 "Full results" links on the page). The source is named at every seam ("Elevation data: komoot", "Cyclingnews 2026 Road National Champions index. View source", "Race dates and winners come from the Wikipedia 2026 season pages the site already reads"). The gap is the first screen: the hero does not say where the season stands. The sentence that does — "58 of 61 WorldTour races run · next: Il Lombardia, 10 Oct" — exists (`calendar-1280.png`, built in `buildSeasonCalendarSection`) but is hidden until the reader presses "Season Calendar"; what the hero says instead is "UCI-INSPIRED RACE DESK" and "RACE RESULTS • SEASON CALENDAR • WATCH THE FINISH • STAGE PROFILES • RACE NEWS • NATIONAL CHAMPIONSHIPS" (`server.js` 11704–11711, 11739).

## 6. Strengths

- **A written honesty rule with visible consequences.** "Do not make the generic case look more realistic; make it look more generic" (`handoff.md`, stage profiles) is the rare design principle that shows up in the pixels: the dashed pictogram box, the hatched nationals windows, "typical, not confirmed" in a legend, "This season on this site" on the rider card, "Not a photograph — nobody in it exists" under the portrait, "The exact day goes here once the 2027 WorldTour calendar is published" in the close-out.
- **Decisions were made on comps with real data, and the site says so.** The almanac, the profile palette, the news line, the rider card and the close-out each cite the mockup they were chosen from; the "Finished-card jerseys stay stacked" and "no plausible silhouette" rejections are recorded with their reason. That is why those elements are the well-executed ones.
- **Colour means something.** Blue top edge = WorldTour card, rainbow edge = a Worlds card (`.card[data-championship="worlds"]::before`), the rainbow stripe under the hero, the yellow outline and tag on the "This week" button, the profile fill mapping height to the same five stripes. The palette (`--uci-blue`, `--uci-yellow`, `--uci-red`, `server.js` 11824–11839) is small and used with intent.
- **The page reorders itself around the fan's week.** `isWorldChampionshipWeek` moving the Worlds to the top and its button to the front of the menu with a tag, a live race pinned once in the phone month list, the final-day rule ("stays under Live until the last stage result is in"), the rest-day pill — the calendar drives the layout.
- **The release notes are the best writing on the site** and an unusual thing to publish at all: dated, short, about the reader ("A Refresh results button … for when you suspect you are looking at an old copy, for instance a tab your phone brought back from earlier in the day").
- **Emptiness is handled by absence, not placeholders.** `buildCompetitionBlock` returns nothing for an empty list (`server.js` 10691–10694), so a section with nothing today vanishes rather than saying "No data"; the off-season gets a letter instead of an empty grid; a Worlds event before its page exists gets an upcoming card with start time, distance and laps. The one exception is the GC line "The general classification is not available yet." (10176), which is fine.
- **The almanac's status card** ("Essentially complete · 83 of 105 federations · 291 titles decided · Next window: January 2027") is a model of telling the reader what a quiet section means.

## 7. Double down / drop

**Double down (ranked)**

1. **The season as the spine.** The calendar already knows where the year is; let the hero say it, let the "Up next" column lead the off-season, and let the close-out letter be the fourth season state (live → results → upcoming → closed). Reason: the reader's first question is "where are we", and today the first screen answers "what features do you have" (C2).
2. **The honesty rule as house style, beyond graphics.** Same-time rows say so (C3); a rider card with nothing to boast about says the best it saw (C4); a finished race's news line leads with the result (C6). Reason: the site's identity is candour, and the reader notices most where the rule is missing — a blank time beside a name.
3. **One voice: the release-notes register everywhere.** Rewrite the ~10 reader-facing strings on the results page and the warm-up page in the voice of `data/release-notes.md` (C1). Reason: this is the cheapest change with the largest effect on how the site feels, and the maker already writes it well.

**Drop or tone down (ranked)**

1. **The hero tagline feature list** ("RACE RESULTS • SEASON CALENDAR • WATCH THE FINISH • …"). It is a menu of nouns; the menu of buttons beside it already is the menu. Replace with the status line.
2. **"UCI-Inspired Race Desk".** "Inspired" hedges, and the stripe already says UCI. The site has a name; the eyebrow can say what the page is today ("Worlds week · Montréal").
3. **Layout-describing section copy** ("arranged in a three-column grid on larger screens"). Say what is in the block, or say nothing — `buildCompetitionBlock` already omits empty blocks, so the description is doing no work the heading does not.
4. **Per-panel km/mi toggles** (C8). Once per card is enough; the preference is remembered.
5. **Do not add the committee to every card.** One line on the page, by hand, is the right dose; the About page earns its laugh because the results page is straight.

## 8. Recheck procedure (monthly, under 45 minutes)

1. **Fetch (5 min).** `curl -sS -o home.html https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app/`, and `/about`, `/release-notes`. Note the byte size of `/` and `gzip -c home.html | wc -c`; drift is more than +20% month on month without a new feature to explain it (C9).
2. **Render (10 min).** Rewrite `/assets/` to `file://` paths into the repo's `assets/` folder; screenshot at 1280×900 and 1280×5000; for a phone, inject `html,body{width:390px}` and render at `--window-size=500` (Chrome's minimum), then crop to 390. Remove `hidden` from `#season-calendar` for the calendar. Look at: hero, first three cards, one stage race card with a measured profile and one with a pictogram, the calendar, the nationals section, the footer.
3. **Read (10 min).** The hero eyebrow and tagline (`server.js` `heroSubheader`), the six block descriptions (`recentBlockDescription`, the "Upcoming" and "Live Multi-Stage" strings in `buildCompetitionSection`), the footer line, the warm-up paragraph, and the newest five release-notes entries.
4. **Probe the honesty rule (10 min).** Pick one finished stage race: does any podium row show a blank where the source says same time? Any profile that looks like a real trace without "Elevation data: komoot"? Any nationals band drawn solid? Hover (in a real browser) one rider with no podium and one team name in a team classification: what does the card say?
5. **Check the season state (5 min).** Which state is the hero in (results / Worlds week / close-out / rollover) and does it match the calendar's own status line? In October–January, read the close-out letter aloud: every number must match the calendar, and the "First results" chip must say a month, not a guessed day, until the 2027 pages carry one.

**What drift toward generic looks like:** a feature list or "the fastest way to…" claim in the hero; a fourth colour or a second gradient in the palette; a section description that mentions columns, grids, or caches; an empty state that says "No data available"; a stage profile that looks like a course when its caption does not name a source; a "typical" band restyled as a date; a release-note entry that describes a fix ("refactored the parser") instead of a change for the reader; a rider card, news line or podium row that prints a placeholder instead of saying what is known. **What drift the other way looks like:** committee jokes on result cards, a mascot in the hero, or the About disclaimers going missing.
