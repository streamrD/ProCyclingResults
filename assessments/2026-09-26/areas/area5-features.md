# Area 5: Feature Opportunities

Assessed commit: 9dc326f. Date: 2026-09-26. Site: https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app. Read-only assessment; nothing was edited, committed or pushed.

## 1. Summary

The site serves Job 2 (see results daily, as soon as they are available) far better than Jobs 1 and 3: live races rebuild every minute, results land about four minutes after the finish, and every results card carries a stage strip, jerseys, top fives, a finish video, a news line and full-results links. The biggest unmet value is on the other side of "today": an upcoming WorldTour card is the least-designed element on the page (kicker, title, "10 October 2026 • Italy"), nothing on the page says that Il Lombardia is a Monument or that only three races remain, the season calendar that knows all of this is hidden behind a button by design, and after 19 October the reader has twelve weeks with no race ahead of them at all. Almost everything needed to fix this is already in the payload: `seasonCalendar` carries ISO dates, tier, anchor and winner for every race, `partitionRaceBuckets` already knows today in UTC, and the Worlds upcoming card proves the richer card shape exists in `buildUpcomingDetailLine`. I recommend three small features that all reuse fetched data: (1) upcoming cards that sell the race (tier badge, countdown, duration, and last year's winner from the previous season's two Wikipedia pages), (2) an iCal feed of the season calendar plus a per-card "Add to calendar" link, and (3) a one-line "This week" strip under the hero, which needs the maintainer to relax the 2026-09-04 "nothing calendar-related until asked" rule by one line. The next two, an RSS results feed and a "new since your last visit" marker, serve Job 2 for a weekend's work and no new upstream. The features to decline are the ones that need a source or state the project has ruled out: a live stage timeline (no source that is not ProCyclingStats or an obscured race-centre API), email or web push (subscription state with no database), rider and team pages (ProCyclingStats does them better and every name already links there), and share images per result (no raster rendering without a package).

## 3. Under-served jobs

**Job 1, a picture of the race landscape ahead.** The only place the site draws the landscape is the season calendar, and by the 2026-09-04 product rule it is hidden until the reader presses "Season Calendar"; the one sentence that summarises the state of the year ("58 of 61 WorldTour races run · next: Il Lombardia, 10 Oct", built in `buildSeasonCalendarSection`) sits inside that hidden section, and the visible hero carries a static tagline ("RACE RESULTS • SEASON CALENDAR • …") that never changes. A reader arriving on the 26th of September sees the Worlds section first (correct this week, via `isWorldChampionshipWeek`) but has to scroll past twelve men's and twelve women's result cards to learn that Il Lombardia and two Chinese races are all that remain. There is no "this week" or "this month" view, no count of races left, and nothing distinguishes a Monument or Grand Tour on a card even though `getSeasonCalendarTier` already classifies every race. The `riderSeasons` index (35 KB of JSON already embedded in the page) holds wins and podiums per rider and nation for the season, and no view summarises it.

**Job 2, results daily, as soon as they are available.** This is the job the site does well and where the release notes show most of the season's work: one-minute rebuilds in racing hours, a Refresh button that knows the next rebuild time, `finishedToday`, the stage strip, per-stage videos, jersey holders and top fives, full-results links, and a news line that refreshes every fifteen minutes. What it lacks is any way to be told rather than to look: no RSS or Atom feed, no calendar subscription, and (by constraint) no push or email. A reader who comes back after two days faces a twelve-card grid with no marker of what is new: cards carry no ISO date attribute (`data-race-date` and the like do not exist), the only "Today" text is the Worlds detail line, and `finishedToday` is not rendered on one-day cards. Finished stage races still lack per-stage finish videos (`enrichStageFinishVideos` is gated to live races; an open thread since August). The page is 1.36 MB of HTML (330 KB of inline SVG for the calendar and championship map, 80 KB CSS, 79 KB JS, 35 KB rider JSON), so a phone reader who only wants the podium pays for everything else, and there is no dark mode for evening reading (`prefers-color-scheme` does not appear in the stylesheet).

**Job 3, excitement about what is next.** This is the weakest job. `buildUpcomingCard` renders a kicker, a title and "date • location" and nothing else for a WorldTour race; `buildUpcomingDetailLine` can add start time, distance and laps, but only the Worlds parser fills those fields, so "Elite men's road race — Start 09:00 local · 273.4 km · 12 laps" sits on the same page as "Il Lombardia — 10 October 2026 • Italy". No countdown, no tier badge, no number of stages, no previous winner, no route, no news (the `/api/race-news` handler searches only `liveStageRaces`, `recentResults` and `finalizedStageRaces`, so an upcoming card cannot even ask), and nowhere for a newer fan to learn what a Monument is or what the jerseys mean beyond the swatch labels. After the last WorldTour race on 18 October the "Upcoming" block is dropped silently (`buildCompetitionBlock` returns nothing for an empty list) and until the season rolls over a week before the 2027 Tour Down Under (`SEASON_ROLLOVER_LEAD_DAYS`, around 9 January) the only forward-looking element is the close-out note's "First results" chip. That is twelve weeks in which the site, by design, has no next race to be excited about, even though the 2027 season pages carry dates from the autumn and `probeSeasonOpening` already reads them.

### Recommended first three

**1. F1: Upcoming cards that sell the race (Job 3, then Job 1).**
Rationale: the upcoming card is the element that fails Job 3, the fix uses data the page already holds, and the Worlds card shows the shape already works. Do it before 10 October so Il Lombardia goes out as "Monument · in 14 days · Last year: …".
Build outline:
- In `buildUpcomingCard(race)`: add a tier chip from `getSeasonCalendarTier(race)` ("Monument", "Grand Tour", or "6-day stage race" from the day count between `race.startDate` and `race.endDate`; "One-day race" needs no chip).
- Countdown: compute days from `race.startDate` against the same `todayUtc` convention `partitionRaceBuckets` uses; render "Tomorrow", "In 14 days", "Today" (the Worlds detail line already prints "Today" from `finishedToday`, so merge into `buildUpcomingDetailLine`). The page is rendered per request from the cached payload, so a day-level countdown is safe; hours are not (the payload can be 15 minutes old and the reader's zone is unknown).
- Last year's winner: in `buildRaceMetadata`, after the season pages, fetch `getSeasonSources(SEASON_YEAR - 1)` through `fetchWikiRaw` and `parseSeasonRows`, cache the result for the process (a settled season's page does not change; a miss or a failure leaves the field empty), join on `race.title` and `race.series`, and attach `previousWinner`, `previousWinnerCountryCode`, `previousWinnerPageTitle` to the upcoming races before `partitionRaceBuckets`. Render through `buildRiderLinkMarkup` so the name is a link and the flag comes from the existing helpers. Add the field to `applyCanonicalRiderNames`'s rename list so the settled spelling wins where the rider also appears this season.
- Tests: a `buildUpcomingCard` test with a fixed `now` and one with a race missing from the previous season. Update the Wikipedia row and add a dated review-log line in `DATA-SOURCES.md` with the measured count (+2 requests per process). Release note: one bold lead.

**2. F2: iCal feed plus "Add to calendar" (Jobs 3 and 1).**
Rationale: a subscription is the cheapest way to make the calendar follow the reader instead of hiding behind a button, it costs no upstream request, and because it is built from `seasonCalendar` it rolls over to 2027 with `resolveSeasonYear` and keeps serving Job 3 through the twelve-week winter gap.
Build outline:
- A `buildSeasonCalendarIcs(calendar, extraRaces, { raceId })` renderer: `VCALENDAR` with `PRODID`, `X-WR-CALNAME: Pro Cycling Results — 2026 WorldTour`, one `VEVENT` per `seasonCalendar.races[]` entry: `UID:<id>@procyclingresults`, `DTSTAMP` from `fetchedAt`, `DTSTART;VALUE=DATE:<startDate>`, `DTEND;VALUE=DATE:<endDate + 1 day>`, `SUMMARY:<title> (<series>, Monument)`, `LOCATION`, `DESCRIPTION` with the winner once `status` is `finished`, `URL:https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app/#<anchor>`. Add the four Worlds events from `allRaces` (series `WORLD_CHAMPIONSHIPS.label`), which `SEASON_CALENDAR_SERIES` excludes from the calendar. A `foldIcsLine` helper (75 octets) and `escapeIcsText` (`,`, `;`, `\`, newlines).
- Route `/calendar.ics` beside `/api/races` in the request handler: `Content-Type: text/calendar; charset=utf-8`, `Cache-Control: public, max-age=3600`; during warm-up answer 503 with `Retry-After` (never an empty calendar, which clients would cache); `?race=<id>` returns one event for the card link; optional `?series=men|women|worlds`.
- UI: an "Add to calendar" link in `buildUpcomingCard`, a "Subscribe (iCal)" link in the calendar section header and the footer; a `SHARE_VIEWS`-style description is not needed.
- Tests: fold and escape helpers, DTEND exclusivity, the Worlds inclusion, the 503 during warm-up. `DATA-SOURCES.md`: a dated "no change to request counts" line, as the rider-links entries did.

**3. F5: A "This week" line under the hero (Job 1).**
Rationale: Job 1 is served only by a hidden section; one line of text under "Updated …" answers "what is on this week and what is next" for every visitor from data `buildSeasonCalendar` already computes. It is the one recommendation that needs a product answer first: the 2026-09-04 rule that nothing calendar-related takes space until asked. The `compact` option of `buildSeasonCalendarSvg` was kept "for a future teaser", which suggests the door is open; a text line is the smaller ask and reads on a phone. If the maintainer says no, promote F4 into this slot.
Build outline:

## 6. Decline list

- **F19 Live stage timeline.** Attractive and squarely Job 2, but there is no source the project may use: ProCyclingStats is linked to and never fetched, the race-centre APIs are obscured (recorded in the feature map), and minute-level polling of anything else would rewrite the `DATA-SOURCES.md` promise. Decline until an organiser publishes an open timing feed.
- **F20 Email and web push.** Needs subscriptions that survive a redeploy, and the project has no database, one instance and no accounts. F3 (RSS) and F2 (iCal) are the honest substitutes: pull channels that need no state.
- **F12 Rider pages and F13 team pages.** Every rider name already links to a fuller ProCyclingStats page, and the site's own index is deliberately partial ("as this site holds them"); a page of its own would look wrong where the hover card looks modest. Teams do not exist in the data model at all.
- **F15 share images per result (the image half).** No raster rendering without a package; SVG is not accepted as an Open Graph image by most platforms. Keep the text-preview half as a low-priority S.
- **F18 JSON API contract.** Serves other projects, not the three reader jobs, and a frozen contract is a standing cost for a solo maintainer. At most, add CORS and a README note that the payload shape is best-effort.
- **F21 podcast and video round-ups.** New source rows for editorial value the "Watch the finish" link and the news line already approximate; a curated links section on the guide page (F9) gets most of it for nothing.
- **F22 language options.** Every string is inline in `server.js`; the cost is a rewrite and the readership follows the English press.
- **F14 past-season archive, for now.** Medium value, but F1's previous-winner line captures the most-asked question ("who won last year") for a fraction of the cost, and the season parser has only met 2026's page layout. Revisit in the off-season if the maintainer wants a "look back" that outlives the close-out note.
- **F16 dark mode, for now.** Real value for evening reading, but the stylesheet has 174 literal colours beside its 15 tokens, plus SVG fills and a palette decision on the profile gradient; it is a weekend of careful work with a whole-page regression surface, not an evening. Schedule it for the winter when nothing is live.

## 8. Recheck procedure

A monthly feature check-in (the last Tuesday, when the committee meets) should take under an hour and ask:

1. **What did readers ask for?** GitHub issues, anything the maintainer's friends said, and Umami: pageviews per path (`/`, `/calendar`, `/championships`, `/about`, `/release-notes`, and `/calendar.ics` and `/feed.xml` once they exist), referrers, and custom events if question 5 is answered yes. Record the three most-used and least-used interactions.
2. **Which upcoming cards lacked context?** One GET of `/`; for each `.upcoming-card`, note whether it carries a tier chip, a countdown, a last-year line and a news line (`upcoming-detail`, once F1 ships). List the races that rendered as title-and-date only and why (no previous edition, no article, no news).
3. **What empty states appeared?** Grep the same HTML for "not available yet", for a section whose Upcoming or Recent block vanished (`buildCompetitionBlock` returns nothing for an empty list), for an empty "Up next" column, and, in the winter, whether the close-out hero shows an exact "First results" day or still the month.
4. **What was the landscape at a glance?** From the hero alone (without opening the calendar), could a reader say what is live, what is next and how many races remain? If F5 shipped, is the line right on this day (rest day, Worlds week, off-season)?
5. **What is the season doing next month?** The Tour route reveal (October), the Giro's (November), the Vuelta's (December or January), the 2027 rollover (around 9 January, with the Cyclingnews address and Worlds article to check), the first races (Tour Down Under). Decide whether F25 (route preview) or F23 (winter upcoming) is due.
6. **Did the sources table stay true?** Re-read the `DATA-SOURCES.md` table against every feature shipped this month; any change to what is fetched must have a dated review-log line with a measured count. The two feeds, if shipped, add nothing upstream and should say so.
7. **Re-rank.** Move shipped items out of the shortlist, promote anything the reader questions or empty states point at, and re-ask the open questions above that are still open. Add one line to `handoff.md` saying what the check-in learned.
