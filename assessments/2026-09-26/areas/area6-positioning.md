# Area 6: Competitive positioning

Assessed commit `9dc326f`, 2026-09-26 (Worlds week in Montréal; the elite women's road race was ridden today, the men's is tomorrow). Read-only; nothing was edited, committed or pushed.

## 1. Summary

The maintainer's hypothesis holds on calm and on in-site findability, and it is unproven against the two sites it most needs to beat. Measured against what could be fetched, this site is the only one in the set where today's result sits on the front page with no tap, no ad slot, no consent manager and one external script: the women's road race podium (Vollering, Niewiadoma, Longo Borghini) is in the leading section of `/`, while Cyclingnews needs one tap into a 2.3 MB live blog that carries a Sourcepoint consent manager and nine ad units, Flashscore's cycling page is a 713 KB JavaScript shell with a consent manager and twenty ad containers, and the UCI's own site keeps results in a client-rendered tab literally named `results0` behind a navigation bar with no "Results" item. ProCyclingStats, FirstCycling and La Flamme Rouge, however, answer every automated fetch with a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403, "Just a moment"), so their ad load and tap paths are recorded here as unverified; the hypothesis about "the big players" rests on Cyclingnews and Flashscore until someone checks those two in a browser. Speed is a draw on bytes and a win on requests: our HTML is one 1.36 MB document (154 KB gzipped, 13,183 DOM nodes, 237 inline SVGs) against Cyclingnews's 1.9 to 2.3 MB plus 84 to 94 scripts and up to 99 images. The real edge is narrower and stronger than "findability": this is the only front page in the set that does all three reader jobs at once, for men and women on equal footing, with nothing to navigate. The weakest point is the mirror image: the site itself cannot be found (a search for its name returns ProCyclingStats, the `@procyclingresults` Instagram handle belongs to someone else, the domain is a PaaS subdomain), and the sourcing and ad-free stance that should carry its trust story is not on the page at all: `DATA-SOURCES.md` lives only on GitHub and the About page never says where a result comes from.

## 3. Competitor profiles

**ProCyclingStats (procyclingstats.com).** Could not be fetched: both curl and WebFetch received a Cloudflare challenge on `/` and on the women's Worlds result page. Search titles show the depth: a race page, a result page, a "LiveStats" live-timing page, a startlist, per-rider profiles and the "Latest race results" calendar page (`/calendar/latest-results`). This is the reference database for the sport and the site ours links to 995 times. Ads, consent and front-page layout: could not verify from here; check in a browser (see section 8). It ranks first for "cycling results today" and, tellingly, first for our own name.

**FirstCycling (firstcycling.com).** Also Cloudflare-blocked (403 on `/`). Search titles show per-race pages (`race.php?r=9067` is the women's Worlds road race), a separate mobile site under `/m/`, rankings and history. Second for "cycling results today". Ads and layout: could not verify.

**Cyclingnews (cyclingnews.com).** Front page 1,925,774 bytes, 84 scripts (17 external), 99 images, a Sourcepoint consent manager (`__tcfapi` ×15, a consent gate for EU visitors), a DFP leaderboard container and four `ad-unit` widgets. Today's result: one tap from the front page, but the tap lands on a live blog ("As it happened: Dutch star secures first road title…", 2,302,030 bytes, 94 scripts, nine distinct ad-unit classes including `live-feed-ad-unit`) and the reader scrolls to a "Top 10 – elite women road race" table. The "Race results" hub (`/race-results/`, 1,559,220 bytes) is a list of articles, not tables. A race calendar exists (`/races/calendar/`). RSS feeds are advertised (2 links). Women's racing is a section of its own and today's women's race led the front page. Strength: reporting, bylines, previews, "how to watch"; the thing ours cannot do.

**VeloViewer (veloviewer.com).** Not a competitor for this reader. The front page is a 16,284-byte landing page with no scripts for a Strava-analytics product; `/pro` (18,276 bytes) is a "Welcome" page. Its WorldTour race package is sold to teams only; its own blog says race routes are not public and points readers to La Flamme Rouge. Zero ads, zero consent markers, zero results.

**UCI (uci.org).** Front page 71,165 bytes, no ad units (the two `googletag` hits are Tag Manager), 8 scripts. The navigation bar has no "Results" entry (Federations, Teams, Officials, Riders, Media, Organisers, The UCI, Regulations, WCC…). Results live in a race hub whose tabs are named `results0`, `results1`, `results2` and `women-elite-itt0`; the results tab (95,689 bytes) contains no rider names in its HTML, so it needs JavaScript before anything shows, as does the calendar page (79,669 bytes, no race names in the markup). It is the authoritative source and has full official result sheets, but a reader finds today's result only by knowing the hub exists.

**La Flamme Rouge (la-flamme-rouge.eu).** Cloudflare-blocked. Search titles show what it is: route maps and profiles for professional races, a climbs map, a race calendar, a forum and route-design contests, with a Telegram chat. It serves Job 1 (the landscape ahead, at route level) better than anyone and does not do Job 2.

**Escape Collective (escapecollective.com).** Front page 511,038 bytes, 29 scripts, 231 images, no consent manager, no ad units; 102 `post__paywall-badge` elements and "Member Benefits" in the header: member-funded journalism. Worlds coverage is previews and reaction pieces; there are no result tables. It is the nearest thing to "calm" among the big players, and it proves calm is purchasable, but it does not do Job 2 and much of it is behind the membership.

**Flashscore (flashscore.com/cycling).** 712,751 bytes, 86 scripts, 20 ad containers, a consent manager (15 markers); the HTML holds no results at all (0 mentions of today's winner), so the page is a shell filled by JavaScript. It ranks for our name. Live scores are its strength; calm is not.

## 5. Positioning statement, and where not to compete

**For** recreational road-cycling fans who want to know what happened today and what is next, **Pro Cycling Results is** a one-page race desk for the men's and women's WorldTour, the Worlds and the national championships **that** shows the podium, the standings, the finish and the next race side by side, in one scroll. **Unlike** ProCyclingStats, FirstCycling and Cyclingnews, which hold far more but ask the reader to navigate a database or a news site through ads and consent banners, **it** carries no advertising, no tracking beyond a page counter, and says where every result comes from. **The reason to believe:** the day's podium is on the front page (zero taps, measured today), the whole page is one document with one external script, and every card links to the full placings on ProCyclingStats rather than pretending to be them.

Where the site cannot and should not compete, and what that implies:

- **Depth of history.** The season is the unit (`FIRST_SEASON_YEAR` is 2026 and the year rolls forward, never back). Do not build archives; say on About that the site is "this season, and only this season", and let the close-out note be the archive.
- **Rider databases and profiles.** The hover card's season tally is the ceiling. Keep names as links to PCS; never add rider pages, and say so, because a reader who wants a palmarès should not look for one here.
- **Live timing and full results.** PCS LiveStats and the organisers' sites own this. "Full results" links are the product's honesty, not a gap; keep them, but label the destination (see P8).
- **Startlists, rankings, transfers, journalism.** Cyclingnews and Escape do these with people. The news line is a pointer to them, not a rival; do not grow it into a coverage block again.
- **Routes and climbs.** La Flamme Rouge. The ASO-only elevation trace rule (a pictogram and "no profile available" elsewhere) is the right boundary.

For the About page this means adding, beneath the committee bios, one plain paragraph that says what the site is, what it is not, where the results come from, that it carries no ads and no tracking beyond a page counter, and that it is not affiliated with the UCI or any organiser. The bios are charming; the paragraph is what a stranger needs to trust the page.

## 7. Open questions for the maintainer

1. Is being found a goal at all? `DATA-SOURCES.md` says "he and a few friends"; the hypothesis in this assessment says "win on findability". Those are different products, and P1, P5 and P9 only matter for the second.
2. Would you rather grow the name "Pro Cycling Results" (generic, and search already gives it to PCS) or move the brand to the Grupetto Committee, which is the distinctive thing you have?
3. Is "UCI-Inspired Race Desk" deliberate? If it is a design homage, the About page should say so in words; if it is a leftover, P3 is a one-line change.
4. Does the deployed site send `Content-Encoding`? One header request answers P4.
5. Would an Atom feed sit comfortably with "we do not store personal data"? It stores nothing about readers, but it is a new thing the site publishes, so `DATA-SOURCES.md` should say so.
6. Have you looked at ProCyclingStats and FirstCycling on a phone with an ad blocker off, recently? This assessment could not, and the hypothesis is about them.

## 8. Recheck procedure

Quarterly is enough; the competitors' layouts change slowly and the site's own scope is fixed by season. Do it once more in the week the season rolls over (about 9 January 2027), since the front page changes shape then. Under an hour:

1. **Our pages (5 min).** `curl -sSL -o home.html -w '%{http_code} %{size_download}\n' https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app/` and the same for `/calendar`; `gzip -9c home.html | wc -c`; `curl -sI -H 'Accept-Encoding: gzip, br' https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app/ | grep -i content-encoding`. Confirm zero matches for `googletag|adsbygoogle|__tcfapi`. Note the first race card's date against today's date.
2. **Competitor HTML (10 min).** Re-run `fetch.sh` and `fetch2.sh` from this assessment's scratch folder (copy them somewhere durable; they take a name and a URL each). Record status, bytes, ad and consent counts. A 403 with "Just a moment" is a result, not an error: write it down.
3. **The two blocked sites (10 min, by hand).** Open procyclingstats.com and firstcycling.com on a phone, ad blocker off. Count the ad slots on the front page and on the day's result page, note whether a consent banner appears, and count taps from the front page to the day's result. Screenshot both. This is the only way to test the hypothesis against the sites it names.
4. **Search (10 min).** Four queries in a logged-out browser: the site's name, the domain, "cycling results today", and "<today's race> results". Record whether the site appears in the first page.
5. **Phone render (10 min).** Save `home.html`, rewrite `/assets/` to the repo's absolute path, load it in a 390 px iframe in headless Chrome (the `frame390.html` and `ours-probe.html` files here show how) and screenshot the top, the men's WorldTour and the national championships sections. Headless Chrome will not go below 500 px on its own; the iframe is the trick.
6. **Write the table (10 min).** Update section 4 with the new numbers and mark anything that changed severity.
