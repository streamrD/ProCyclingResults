# Area 3: Audience definition and fit

Assessed commit: 9dc326f · Site: https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app · Date: Saturday 26 September 2026 (Worlds week, Montréal; women's road race today, men's tomorrow)

Scratch evidence directory (screenshots, saved pages, probe scripts): `assessments/tools/area3/` — referred to below as `scratch/`.

## 1. Summary

**Grade: B-** for fit with the approved reader. The site does Job 2 (see results daily, as soon as available) well: a top five with times, a finish video, a full-results link and a news line on every card, a live rebuild every minute, an honest "Updated" timestamp with a refresh button, and this week the Worlds correctly lead the page with a "This week" badge. Job 1 (the landscape ahead) is served by a genuinely good season calendar, but it is hidden behind the last of five buttons, omits the Worlds, and on a phone opens at January. Job 3 (get excited about what is next) is the weakest: an upcoming card is a name, a date and a country, and in the off-season the next season's calendar does not exist on the site until seven days before it starts. What matters most: on a phone the entire first screen is a masthead (hero 729px of an 844px viewport) that says nothing about today, so the reader's one-minute visit starts with a scroll every time; the National Championships section is genuinely clipped at 390px (a real bug, not the headless-Chrome artefact the handoff describes); a rider on the winner's time shows a blank instead of "same time"; and the classification top fives are hover-only, so no phone reader and no keyboard user can ever see them. The reader is never defined anywhere in the repo or on the site, although "the reader" is invoked as if it were. No analytics were available to me; the site does run Umami, so the maintainer can answer the phone-share and section-reach questions in Section 7 without guessing.

## 3. Reader statement and jobs table (proposed for adoption)

**Is the reader defined today?** No. The closest lines are:

- `README.md` l.21: "The app is a live race desk for the current season's selected UCI calendars" — describes the product, not a person.
- `data/about.md`: "offered entirely free, for all to use and enjoy" — everyone, i.e. nobody in particular.
- Homepage `<meta name="description">`: "Race results, the season calendar, finish videos, stage profiles, race news and national champions for the 2026 men's and women's UCI WorldTour."
- `AGENTS.md` (release-notes rule): "Say what changed for the reader, not how it was found or fixed"; `handoff.md` l.1706-1711: "A first answer can be right and still wrong for the reader … use the one readers expect"; `handoff.md` l.1533: "**Results first, always.**" The word "reader" is used as though defined; it never is, and the one product principle that exists ("results first") is a layout rule rather than a reader.

**Proposed reader statement.** The Pro Cycling Results reader is a recreational road cyclist — someone who rides at weekends, watches the Tour and a few classics on television, and follows a handful of riders by name. They know the big races and the big names but not every race's jersey colours, the abbreviations the sport uses (GC, ITT, s.t., RR/TT), the UCI's series structure, or which race is a Monument. They do not want long reports, 150-rider result sheets, fantasy or betting data, or anything they have to learn to read. They visit on a phone, in a spare minute, from a bookmark or a shared link: most days during a Grand Tour or the Worlds, once or twice a week the rest of the season, and a couple of times over the winter to see when it all starts again. They want, in that minute: who won today and by how much, a way to watch the finish, a glance at the standings, and a reason to come back tomorrow.

| Job | What the reader is trying to do | Success measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Job 1 — Picture the landscape ahead** | Know what is on this month and next in both WorldTours and the championships, and where the season is | Within one tap from the first screen, on any day of the year including the off-season, the reader can name the next three races with dates and the next Grand Tour or Monument. Check monthly; Umami event on the calendar button gives the open rate. |
| **Job 2 — See results daily, as soon as they are available** | Find today's winner and top five, watch the finish, see the standings, trust that it is current | On a race day, today's result is on the first screen (phone and desktop), marked as today's, within ten minutes of the finish (handoff measured about four). Measure: `fetchedAt` versus finish time; screen position of today's card; return visits per race day in Umami. |
| **Job 3 — Get excited about what is next** | Look forward to the next race: when, where, how far, why it matters, who to watch | The next race's card gives at least three of: a countdown or weekday, distance/route, why it matters (Monument, Grand Tour, Worlds), a preview story, a finish-video link from last year. Measure: clicks on upcoming cards and calendar bars; visits the day before a race. |

## 6. Strengths

- **Job 2 is close to right.** Top five with times and gaps, "Watch the race finish", "Full results" on ProCyclingStats, and "Latest news" on every card; live rebuild every minute; "Updated" timestamp with a "Refresh results" button that says whether there is anything newer (release notes 5-6 Sep; `data-fetched-at` in the hero).
- **Worlds week is handled as the reader would want**: the section and its button lead the page with a "This week" badge (`server.js` 10627-10633; `scratch/home-1280.png`); on desktop the three medal cards are at the fold.
- **The calendar's header line** — "58 of 61 WorldTour races run · next: Il Lombardia, 10 Oct" — is the single best Job 1 sentence on the site, and the desktop timeline with a TODAY marker, tiers and honest hatched "typical window" bands is the right picture (`scratch/desktop-calendar.png`).
- **Live-day copy is plain English**: "Rest day, 7 September. Stage 15 was raced yesterday; racing resumes tomorrow with Stage 16 …" (`server.js` 9383-9385).
- **Women's racing has structurally equal footing**: identical section shape, same card depth, same "Load more", same calendar lane; the Worlds show men's and women's events in one block.
- **Honest graphics**: generic stage pictograms with a "no elevation profile" note where there is no trace; hatched, labelled "not confirmed" windows.
- **Rider names link out and open a season card on desktop**; Escape closes; the card opens on keyboard focus.
- **About is unambiguous about its fiction** ("Every word of the biographies above is invented. The bakery is real.") and the close-out photo caption repeats it — a first-time reader is not misled, only under-informed (A8).
- **Basics present**: `lang`, viewport, no horizontal page scroll (sections clip rather than scroll), `role="status"` on the refresh line, `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls` on the news toggle, reduced-motion guard on the calendar, no third-party UI code.

## 7. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **Phone share.** Umami is installed (`todd-umami.up.railway.app`, website id `2ad971aa…`; release notes 1 May 2026). What share of visits is on phones? Above ~50% makes A1 and A10 the top priorities; below 20% they drop a level.
2. **Section reach.** Umami's per-section events (if the hero buttons are tracked): how many visits click "Women's WorldTour", "National Championships" and "Season Calendar"? If the calendar button is rarely used, A7's teaser is the fix; if it is used a lot, the phone list order matters more.
3. **Return rhythm.** Daily-active during the Vuelta versus a rest day versus a September Saturday would tell us whether Job 2 (daily) or Job 1/3 (weekly) deserves the hero.
4. **Scroll depth** to the women's section on phones (Umami does not give this by default; a one-line `IntersectionObserver` event would).
5. **Share-link entry**: what fraction of visits land on `/calendar` and `/championships`? Those readers arrive with Job 1 in mind and see a masthead first.
6. Is the men-first order of Worlds results (user request 2026-09-07) still wanted on a day when the women's event is the news?
7. Should the Grupetto voice appear on the results page (it is currently confined to About and the close-out)? The assessment above assumes results stay in the neutral voice.
8. Is "Eastern Time" a deliberate statement about the audience, or an accident of where the maintainer lives?
