# Area 9 — Sourcing and legal footing

Assessed commit: `9dc326f` in `/Users/tcs16/AgenticAI/ProCyclingResults`. Date: 2026-09-26. Live site: https://procyclingresults.up.railway.app.

**This report was written by a software assessor, not a lawyer.** It identifies where the site's fetching and republishing could get it blocked, rate-limited, or asked to stop, and where a maintainer growing the audience should get legal advice. Nothing here is legal advice; section 7 lists the questions that need one.

## 1. Summary

Overall risk of being blocked or asked to stop: **Medium**. The request volume is small, paced, documented in public (`DATA-SOURCES.md`) and carried on a user agent that names the policy page and, when set, a contact address; that is better hygiene than most hobby sites and is the main reason this is not High. Against that, three sources are read in ways their own terms or `robots.txt` say not to: YouTube's search page (`/results` is disallowed in YouTube's robots.txt and its Terms ban automated access), ASO's rankings AJAX partials (`/*/ajax` is disallowed on letour.fr, letourfemmes.fr and lavuelta.es, and ASO's conditions of use forbid robots and data extraction without written consent), and the Cyclingnews nationals index (Future plc's UK terms forbid text and data mining and web scraping outright). Wikipedia is the one source with an explicit reuse licence, and the site's use of it (facts from tables, one revision query, changed pages only, maxlag set) sits comfortably inside Wikimedia's policies; what is missing is a reader-visible attribution line, which is trivial to add. `DATA-SOURCES.md` is mostly accurate but understates the Bing News volume by about three times (it says "about ten searches per race"; the code caps at 32 while a race is live or fresh). The one thing to fix first is the YouTube finish-video search: replace the scrape of `youtube.com/results` with the YouTube Data API (or the curated `RACE_FINISH_VIDEO_URLS` and organiser feeds only), because it is the clearest terms breach, the one most likely to be blocked automatically from a datacenter IP, and the cheapest to replace. Second is a short email to ASO, which `DATA-SOURCES.md` already invites in spirit, asking whether reading the public rankings page four times a minute during a Grand Tour is acceptable to them.

## 6. Strengths

- `DATA-SOURCES.md` exists, is public (the GitHub blob answers HTTP 200 unauthenticated), is the URL in the user agent, invites contact, and keeps a dated review log with measured request counts. Very few hobby sites do this; it is the strongest single argument that the site is a considerate guest.
- The user agent follows the Wikimedia policy format (client name, version, policy URL, contact when `SOURCE_CONTACT` is set) rather than imitating a browser (server.js:66–71).
- Wikipedia is read the way Wikimedia asks: one batched revision query with `maxlag=5`, changed pages only, three-connection ceiling, no retry storms (`FETCH_RETRY_DELAYS_MS` two short waits, 429/5xx only), and an idle-page eviction after a day. Facts, not prose, are republished, which is what the Terms of Use say may be reused freely.
- Finished races are polled every six hours, not every minute (the 2026-09-05 review cut 58 requests per rebuild to 5).
- YouTube videos and ProCyclingStats pages are linked, never embedded or fetched; komoot is attributed under every measured profile; the nationals table names and links its source; Bing headlines carry the publisher's name and link to the publisher, and the description field is parsed but not rendered.
- Race data is derived, not copied wholesale: stage profiles are 120-point series, results are top fives, and the site shows nothing a visitor cannot see on the source.
- No accounts, no cookies, no form data, analytics self-hosted and cookieless; the only browser storage is two display preferences.
- The About page's fictional committee is labelled fictional in three places ("Not a photograph — nobody in it exists", "Every word of the biographies above is invented"), and no real person or organisation is impersonated. Race, team, jersey and UCI names are used as plain descriptive text; no logos or official imagery are bundled (`assets/` holds only the favicon, fonts, the committee illustrations and Open Graph images).

## 7. Questions for a lawyer, and open questions for the maintainer

For a lawyer (the jurisdiction question comes first: the maintainer is in the United States, the host is Railway (US), the sources are French, Spanish, Italian, British and US companies, and the readers are anywhere):

- Q1. Are ASO's browsewrap conditions (letour.fr CGU 3.14–3.15, lavuelta.es Aviso Legal 5 and 7) enforceable against a server that never clicked through, and does reading four public pages a minute of published results fall within any US or EU exception for facts and non-substantial extraction (EU Database Directive 96/9/EC, art. 8; US: facts are not copyrightable, but contract and computer-access claims are separate)?
- Q2. Does Future plc's "no text or data mining, or web scraping" clause reach an hourly read of one page for a non-commercial site, and would the UK's text-and-data-mining exception (CDPA s.29A, non-commercial research only) help or not at all?
- Q3. Is republishing a downsampled elevation series derived from komoot's API, with attribution, in a public repository a reuse of a "tour" under komoot ToS 1.4, given that the site is not a komoot account holder and the organiser chose to embed the tour publicly?
- Q4. If the audience becomes materially EU/UK-based, does a cookieless, self-hosted, anonymised analytics install need anything beyond a privacy statement (GDPR art. 13 notice) — for example, a legal-basis statement or a UK PECR reg. 6 analysis?
- Q5. Is "Pro Cycling Results" clear of "ProCyclingStats" and any registered marks before the maintainer buys a domain or promotes the site?
- Q6. Are YouTube's Terms breached by server-side reading of a results page for the purpose of choosing one link, and does moving to the Data API (which has its own API Services Terms, including display and quota rules) settle that?

## 8. Recheck procedure

Quarterly, under 30 minutes, read-only:

1. **Hosts in code (5 min).** `grep -o 'https://[a-z0-9.-]*' server.js | sort | uniq -c` and `grep -n 'fetchText(\|fetchJson(' server.js`. Every host with a fetch must be a row in the DATA-SOURCES table; any new host triggers a full re-read of that host's terms and robots.txt. Confirm `procyclingstats` never appears in a fetch call.
2. **Cadence constants (5 min).** `grep -n '_TTL_MS\s*=\|_LIMIT\s*=\|MAX_.*QUERIES\|WIKI_FETCH_CONCURRENCY\|FETCH_RETRY_DELAYS_MS' server.js` and compare each value with the table's "When we ask" column. Any changed value triggers a DATA-SOURCES edit and a review-log line.
3. **robots.txt diff (5 min).** `for h in en.wikipedia.org www.letour.fr www.letourfemmes.fr www.lavuelta.es www.lavueltafemenina.es www.tour-auvergne-rhone-alpes.fr www.eschborn-frankfurt.de www.giroditalia.it www.giroditaliawomen.it www.vueltaburgos.com www.bing.com www.youtube.com www.cyclingnews.com www.komoot.com; do curl -s "https://$h/robots.txt" > "robots/$h.txt"; done` and `diff` against the copies saved with this report (the assessor's scratch copies (not kept; the recheck procedure refetches them)). 4. **Terms pages (10 min).** Re-open and search for "robot", "scrap", "automat", "extract", "mining", "link": letour.fr `/fr/mentions-legales` (CGU 3.14–3.15; note the "Mise à jour" date, 23/06/2021 at this check), letourfemmes.fr `/fr/mentions-legales`, lavuelta.es `/es/aviso-legal` (clauses 4, 5, 7), YouTube `/t/terms` ("automated means"), Future `terms-and-conditions-uk` ("NO TEXT OR DATA MINING"), komoot `/terms-of-service` (1.4), Wikimedia User-Agent policy and Robot policy, Umami FAQ.
